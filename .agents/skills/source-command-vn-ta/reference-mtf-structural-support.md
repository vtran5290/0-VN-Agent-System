# Multi-Timeframe Structural Support & Supply/Demand Confluence

**SSOT for chart analysis doctrine** used by `vn-ta-fireant`.  
Applies to: stock screening, market-regime reviews, Wyckoff analysis, entry-timing logic.

**Weekly deep-dive (MA compression, role reversal, LPS, weekly score):**  
see `reference-weekly-structural-support.md`.

**Two-axis scoring (support quality ≠ stock quality; reclaim; 2×2 matrix):**  
see `reference-support-vs-trend.md`.

**Polarity / role reversal (STRUCTURAL_PIVOT_ZONE first; support/resistance contextual):**  
see `reference-polarity-pivot-zone.md`.

**Goal:** distinguish structural support vs tactical support, supply absorption vs false support, good structure vs good entry — not to predict exact levels perfectly.

---

## Key doctrine

> **Zoom out to define structural supply/demand.  
> Zoom in to define timing and confirmation.  
> Never use a short-timeframe chart to decide the magnitude of a long-timeframe supply problem.**

### Permanent principles

> **Strong support comes from confluence, not from one indicator.**

> **A moving average is more meaningful when it overlaps horizontal structure and the origin of markup.**

> **Support is a zone, not an exact line.**

> **Volume tells whether supply is being absorbed.**

> **Oversold does not equal bottom.**

> **Zoom out to define supply. Zoom in to define timing.**

> **A good chart can still be a bad entry.**

> **No-supply must eventually produce upward price response; otherwise it may simply be no-demand.**

> **The first rebound does not prove accumulation; demand must prove control.**

> **Structural context comes first, indicators second.**

## 1. Multi-timeframe analysis hierarchy

Always inspect a stock/index in this sequence:

### A. Monthly chart — structural context

Primary questions:

1. What is the long-term trend?
2. Where are the major historical supply/demand zones?
3. Where did the previous major markup begin?
4. Which old resistance zones later became support?
5. Are MA50M / MA100M / other long-term averages rising, flat, or falling?
6. Is the current decline merely testing long-term structure?
7. Is the security still above its secular trend support?

Use monthly charts to define:

- major structural support,
- major structural resistance,
- secular trend,
- multi-year trapped supply,
- origin of major markup,
- prior breakout zones,
- long-term moving-average confluence.

Monthly should NOT be used for precise entry timing.

### B. Weekly chart — Wyckoff / base structure

Primary questions:

1. Is this accumulation or re-accumulation?
2. Is there a valid trading range?
3. Is there a spring/shakeout?
4. Is there a Sign of Strength?
5. Is there an LPS?
6. Is supply being absorbed?
7. Is the stock in Phase C, D, or E?

Weekly is the main timeframe for:

- accumulation/re-accumulation,
- distribution,
- range quality,
- supply absorption,
- breakout validation,
- Wyckoff phase.

### C. Daily / intraday chart — execution

Primary questions:

1. What is the exact trigger?
2. Is price extended?
3. Where is the short-term invalidation?
4. Is volume confirming the move?
5. Is this a breakout, retest, LPS, or failed breakout?

Daily/intraday charts are for:

- entry,
- stop,
- tactical support,
- breakout confirmation,
- short-term momentum.

Never allow the daily chart to override a major monthly support zone without evidence.

## 2. Strong support is a zone, not a line

Do NOT treat support as a precise price.

Example:

If a monthly SMA50 is 58.3, the correct interpretation may be:

> “Major monthly demand zone approximately 57–60.”

Support strength increases when multiple independent factors cluster in the same area.

This is called **confluence**.

---

## 3. Support confluence framework

For every candidate support zone, evaluate the following independent layers.

### Layer 1 — Higher-timeframe moving average

Examples:

- monthly SMA50,
- monthly SMA100,
- weekly SMA40,
- weekly EMA50,
- daily MA200.

Support is stronger when:

- the moving average is rising,
- price is approaching it for the first time after a long uptrend,
- it aligns with other structural levels.

A moving average alone is NOT sufficient.

### Layer 2 — Historical horizontal pivot

Identify price zones that historically acted as:

- resistance,
- support,
- breakout level,
- major congestion zone.

Strong confluence occurs when:

> old resistance → breakout → later support.

This is classic role reversal.

### Layer 3 — Origin of major markup

Ask:

> “Where did the last major impulsive up-leg begin?”

If a stock rose from 58 → 90 and later returns toward 58–60, that area is important because:

- prior institutional accumulation may have occurred there,
- missed buyers may re-enter,
- previous holders may defend the zone,
- latent demand may remain.

The origin of markup should be treated as a potential institutional demand zone.

### Layer 4 — Prior accumulation base

Identify whether the support zone overlaps:

- a prior multi-week or multi-month base,
- a high-volume consolidation,
- a prior LPS,
- a prior breakout shelf.

The longer the prior base and the cleaner the breakout, the more relevant the zone.

### Layer 5 — Volume behavior during the decline

Do not evaluate support without volume.

Bullish support test:

- price declines sharply,
- volume contracts or does not expand proportionally,
- selling pressure fails to accelerate,
- price begins to stabilize.

Potential interpretation:

> supply is drying up.

Bearish support failure:

- price reaches support,
- volume expands sharply on red bars,
- wide spreads appear,
- close is near lows,
- follow-through selling continues.

Potential interpretation:

> structural support is failing.

### Layer 6 — Momentum reset

Use RSI / StochRSI as context only.

A healthy correction often looks like:

- strong prior uptrend,
- RSI previously overbought,
- RSI resets into neutral or mildly oversold territory,
- price tests major structural support.

Momentum reset strengthens the case that the correction has removed excess speculation.

Do NOT treat RSI oversold alone as a bottom signal.

## 4. Support strength score

Score a support zone using this framework.

### Structural Support Score — 100 points

#### A. Higher-timeframe MA confluence — 20 points

- rising monthly/weekly MA: 10
- price testing it after long separation: 5
- first/second meaningful test: 5

#### B. Horizontal structure — 20 points

- old resistance/support pivot: 10
- repeated historical reactions: 5
- role reversal confirmed: 5

#### C. Origin of markup / prior base — 20 points

- origin of major impulsive move: 10
- prior accumulation base: 5
- prior LPS / breakout shelf: 5

#### D. Volume behavior — 20 points

- sell volume contracts: 10
- no panic expansion near support: 5
- recovery volume improves: 5

#### E. Momentum / trend context — 10 points

- secular trend still intact: 5
- RSI/momentum reset: 5

#### F. Risk / invalidation clarity — 10 points

- clear invalidation zone: 5
- next lower structural support identifiable: 5

Classification:

- 85–100: Exceptional structural support
- 70–84: Strong support
- 55–69: Moderate support
- 40–54: Weak / tentative
- <40: Not meaningful

## 5. Supply absorption framework

A support zone is not automatically bullish.

After identifying the zone, determine whether supply is actually being absorbed.

Look for:

1. Lower selling volume.
2. Narrower down bars.
3. Failed attempts to break support.
4. Long lower wicks.
5. Higher lows.
6. OBV stabilizing or improving.
7. CMF improving.
8. Price refusing to decline despite negative sentiment.
9. Repeated tests with less downside result.
10. Strong rebound from support.

The central principle:

> **Effort vs Result.**

If selling effort is large but downside result is small:

→ possible absorption.

If selling effort is small but price still falls easily:

→ demand is weak.

If selling effort expands and price accelerates lower:

→ support is failing.

## 6. Strong support vs failed support

For each support zone, classify:

### A. Strong Support Candidate

- multiple confluence factors,
- long-term trend intact,
- selling volume not expanding,
- price stabilizing,
- no sustained close below zone.

### B. Support Under Test

- price inside the support zone,
- outcome unresolved,
- require confirmation.

### C. Support Failure

- closes below support zone,
- volume expands,
- no immediate reclaim,
- subsequent rally fails beneath the broken zone.

Once support fails, do not rationalize.

Move to the next structural demand zone.

## 7. Applying the framework to monthly charts

When a user asks:

“Why is 58.3 strong monthly support?”

Do NOT answer:

“Because MA50 is 58.3.”

Instead answer using confluence:

Example structure:

1. Monthly SMA50 is near 58.3 and rising.
2. 58–60 was a prior multi-year resistance/pivot.
3. The same zone became support after breakout.
4. It was the launch area for the latest markup.
5. Current decline is approaching the area with contracting volume.
6. Momentum has reset from overbought.

Conclusion:

> “58.3 is best interpreted as a 57–60 structural demand zone created by multiple independent factors, not as an exact line.”

## 9. Accumulation vs distribution

When evaluating a range, do not use sideways price alone.

### Accumulation / re-accumulation evidence

- pullbacks become shallower,
- down-volume contracts,
- OBV holds or rises,
- price repeatedly rejects lower prices,
- support tests produce less downside,
- resistance eventually breaks on volume,
- LPS forms.

### Distribution / re-distribution evidence

- rallies become weaker,
- up-volume contracts,
- down-volume expands,
- repeated failure near resistance,
- OBV deteriorates,
- support breaks on increasing volume,
- rally after breakdown fails.

Always use:

> price + volume + context + timeframe.

## 10. Do not anthropomorphize “Composite Man”

Wyckoff’s “Composite Man” is a useful abstraction.

Do NOT assume every price move is deliberately manipulated by one operator.

Translate intent-based narratives into measurable behavior.

Instead of:

“Big player pushed price down to shake people out.”

Say:

“Selling pressure appeared, but price did not materially break support; subsequent recovery suggests supply may have been absorbed.”

Always describe observable:

- supply,
- demand,
- effort,
- result,
- absorption,
- failure.

Not imagined intent.

## 12. Example reference case — MWG

Use the following only as a teaching example, not as a permanent price target.

Observed monthly structure:

- Current price near low 60s.
- Monthly SMA50 around 58.
- Prior horizontal pivot around 58–60.
- Prior breakout/base around the same area.
- Major previous markup launched from roughly this area.
- Monthly RSI reset substantially.
- Current decline volume not obviously showing full liquidation.

Correct interpretation:

> “Approximately 57–60 is a strong monthly structural demand zone because a rising monthly SMA50 overlaps a multi-year horizontal pivot, prior breakout shelf, and origin of the previous markup.”

Do NOT say:

> “58.3 must hold because SMA50 is there.”

Failure condition:

- decisive monthly breakdown below the zone,
- expanding selling volume,
- inability to reclaim.

Then move analysis toward the next lower structural demand zone.
