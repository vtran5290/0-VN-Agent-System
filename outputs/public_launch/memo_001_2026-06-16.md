## 6. Framework Behavior Notes

**Downtrend probability model (VNINDEX, v2):**
The rules-based analog model — which compares current VNINDEX price/breadth conditions to historical instances — produces the following readings as of 2026-06-16 (T+10 horizon, shrinkage-adjusted, k=10 nearest analogs):

- **P(confirmed structural downtrend, 20-day horizon):** 13%
- **P(close below 50-day MA proxy, 20-day horizon):** 71%
- **P(trend break, 20-day horizon):** 68%
- **Regime band:** Red (elevated risk zone)

**How to read these numbers:**
The 13% confirmed downtrend figure and the 71% MA50-breach figure are not contradictory — they measure different outcome definitions. "Confirmed downtrend" is a high bar (structural 20-day breakdown analog); "MA50 breach" is a lower bar (a single close below the 50-day MA proxy). The model is saying: a brief test of the MA50 area is historically common from these conditions (~70% base rate), but a sustained structural breakdown is less frequent (~13% base rate).

**Low sample caveat:** k=10 nearest analogs is a small sample. Confidence intervals are wide. These numbers describe historical base rates from similar configurations — they are not probability estimates for this specific week's outcome.

**Methodology note:** The model identifies 10 historical periods with similar market conditions to today, based on price position relative to the 50-day moving average, the slope of that average, and price position relative to the 20-day average. A "confirmed downtrend" is defined as a decline of 7% or more over the next 20 sessions combined with the price closing below the 50-day average for at least two consecutive days. "MA50 breach" and "trend break" use progressively lower bars. The probability estimates draw from all available VNINDEX data since 2012. A shrinkage adjustment blends the observed frequency from the 10 nearest analogs with a baseline rate from the full historical dataset, reducing false confidence when the analog sample is small. Walk-forward calibration tests the model on future periods using only data available at the time of each historical prediction.

**Framework behavior this week:**
The combination of State B regime, Elevated distribution, VN30 below MA20, and Red downtrend band puts the framework in one of its most cautious configurations in recent history. The rules-based screen for new criterion matches has tightened correspondingly.

## 8. Historical Case Study

**Period: Q4 2018 — Divergent breadth ahead of a regime transition**

*All observations are from historical data. This case is ≥6 months old and uses multi-stock sector observations, not individual current signals.*

**What happened:**
In Q4 2018, the VNINDEX experienced a period of elevated distribution (5–7 distribution days in rolling 20-session windows) while the domestic monetary environment remained relatively accommodative. The VN30 index underperformed broader breadth measures, with mid and small-cap stocks holding up better on a relative basis — a pattern structurally similar to current conditions.

**What the framework observed at the time:**
The regime classifier held in State B (global tightening from Fed rate hikes, domestic liquidity still easing) through much of Q3–Q4 2018. Breadth conditions deteriorated progressively from Q3 onward — distribution days accumulated while new high counts declined. The RS framework showed banking and consumer sectors maintaining relative strength longer than energy and industrial names.

**Outcome in the data:**
In the full 2018 drawdown, VNINDEX fell 26.2% from its April 9 peak of 1,204 to its October 30 low of 889. Within the narrower Q3–Q4 window, the decline from October 4 to October 30 was 13.2% — the late-2018 move should be understood as the continuation of a broader 2018 bear-market drawdown, not a standalone Q3–Q4 event. The regime eventually reclassified to Bear (State C) as global tightening propagated through to domestic conditions in late 2018. The breadth deterioration — elevated distribution days + VN30 below trend MA — preceded the regime reclassification by approximately 4–6 weeks in the historical data.

**What this case study does NOT imply:**
This historical parallel is presented as structural context — not a forecast. The 2026 setup shares some observable criteria with Q4 2018 (elevated distribution, VN30 below MA20, global tight / VN easing configuration) but the analogy breaks down in multiple dimensions: global monetary cycle position differs, Vietnam credit growth trajectory differs, and the domestic policy response capacity differs. Pattern similarity is not pattern repetition.
