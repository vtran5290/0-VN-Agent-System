### U1. Vietnam Growth Target Defended Despite Hormuz Risk

**FACTS:**
- `[SCENARIO]` ADB projected that prolonged Hormuz/Middle East energy disruption could reduce Developing Asia-Pacific GDP by up to **1.3 percentage points** over 2026–2027 and add **3.2 percentage points** to inflation if disruption lasts more than one year. (Source: ADB statement — forward scenario, not a confirmed event.)
- `[FACT]` Vietnam's net oil import is approximately **2% of GDP**. Petroleum reserve coverage is approximately **1–2 months of imports**.
- `[FACT]` Vietnam leadership stated the country **will not lower its growth target** and remains committed to becoming a high-income developed country by 2045.
- `[FACT]` Long-term logic: Vietnam needs to sustain approximately **10%+ growth** to meet the 2045 ambition.

**INFERENCE:**
- This is a policy reaction-function signal, not a single-stock catalyst.
- Increases probability of stronger policy push across: public investment, selective credit, capital-market development, SOE restructuring, FDI attraction, strategic infrastructure, energy security, social and rental housing, digital transformation.

**RISKS (per council review):**
- Growth-target defense increases overheating probability: CPI above target, wider trade deficit, FX pressure, higher bank funding costs.
- **SBV rate-cut runway is closing**: CPI +5.60% YoY in May already exceeds the 4.5% SBV target. Sustained above 5% = no broad easing.
- **VND depreciation pressure**: trade deficit -USD 13.8bn 5M + foreign outflow -USD 2.433bn YTD = compounding FX risk. VNDIRECT 2026F USD/VND: 27,100.
- Real consumption weakness (real retail +6.3% after inflation) is not addressed by growth-target defense.

**PM REGIME IMPLICATION:** Regime updated to "Pro-growth stance maintained despite macro headwinds — selective capital-formation risk-on, narrow and flow-fragile, macro-stability constrained."

**AFFECTED SECTORS:** Public investment / construction volume; energy security; quality/policy banks; capital-market development; FDI/KCN/logistics.

### U2. PM Directs MoF to Submit Stock-Market Development Proposal in June

**FACTS:**
- Prime Minister requested MoF to submit a **stock-market development proposal in June 2026**.
- Proposal scope: stock-market development, investment-fund mobilization, continued SOE equitization, international financial-center products, foreign-investment attraction, bottleneck resolution in large projects.
- SBV directed to: maintain market liquidity, stabilize rates, assess deposit/lending structure, coordinate fiscal/monetary solutions, arrange credit for key national projects, develop rental-housing funding plans.
- MoF directed to run **reasonably expansionary fiscal policy** with selective tax/fee/land-rent support. Healthcare/education price increases to be tightly controlled.

**INFERENCE:**
- This is a capital-market policy put signal. Vietnam is trying to mobilize capital through equity market, bond market, funds, SOE equitization and financial-center products — because CPI/FX limits broad monetary easing.

**RISKS:**
- This is a **directive to draft a proposal**, not an implemented policy. The real catalyst is the June proposal content.
- If the proposal is broad but lacks concrete mechanisms (timelines, quotas, incentives), the market narrative will front-run and disappoint.
- MSCI still has FX hedging / capital-flow gaps that one directive cannot resolve.
- Broker/SOE upgrade is premature at directive stage.

**WATCHLIST CHANGES:**
- Brokers (SSI, HCM, VCI, VND, VIX, SHS, MBS): moved to **Watch — pending June proposal content**. NOT upgraded as direct beneficiaries yet. Differentiate by balance-sheet quality: SSI/HCM/VCI > VIX/SHS on margin-book discipline.
- SOE free-float names (PLX, GAS, BSR, ACV, VGC): **Watch** — monitor actual MoF/CMSC divestment follow-through.
- Construction volume (HPG, CTD, BMP, VCG, FCN, HHV): **Delayed beneficiaries** if rental/social housing funding is implemented.

### U6. Final PM Stance — 03 Jun 2026

The June 3 policy and corporate updates strengthen the PM thesis that Vietnam is moving from bank-credit-led growth toward a broader capital-market, SOE-free-float, selective-credit and construction-volume regime.

## 4. POLICY CHANGES

### 4.1 SBV / Credit Policy
- FACT: Policy rate 4.50%, unchanged.
- FACT: SBV maintained supportive liquidity via OMO (+VND 22.7tn net May injection; VND 332.3tn outstanding).
- FACT: Interbank overnight 6.00–7.00%, tighter MoM.
- FACT: HSC noted chatter of possible SBV guidance to halt credit expansion through end-Q2 2026.

- INTERPRETATION: SBV is supportive on liquidity but not broad easing. Aggressive credit-growth banks face quota-cut risk. Quality/funding banks (ACB, VCB, CTG) become relatively more attractive.

### 4.2 Selective Real-Estate Credit Exclusion
